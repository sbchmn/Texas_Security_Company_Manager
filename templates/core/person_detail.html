A step that names a record type or a requirement is checked against the personnel file: it cannot be marked complete while that evidence is missing, and an archived record does not count. Waiving is reserved for the roles that file records — “this does not apply to me” is a decision about an insurance condition or a state requirement, not the officer's to make — and a waiver keeps the reason next to it. A step with no hire date on the profile shows no hire date rather than a date invented from today, because an invented deadline turns a data-entry gap into somebody being chased for being late.

A registry check is a record that somebody opened the DPS licence search and wrote down what it said — there is no public API, so this is the strongest claim the product can make, and it is dated and attributed rather than a tick. A check that is simply old does not move this officer's compliance rate: it says the office has not looked, not that the licence is bad. A check that came back expired or not found does count as attention, because then we looked. Recording a clean check marks the credential verified; it never un-verifies one, and it never changes a status the office set itself.
